\subsubsection{Effect of noise on the input data}

So far, we have studied convergence aspects regarding the data-driven formulation without introducing noise into the given data field $(\tilde{\eD}, \tilde{\sD})$. In the present section, in light of the proposed manufactured solution to Darcy's problem, we introduce a noise distribution in the original data field; in particular, a simple normal (or Gaussian) distribution was selected to introduce the random noise in to the data. The characteristics of the standard normal distribution will be defined by its mean and standard deviation, which will be denoted by $\mu_{\text{gn}} $ and $\sigma_{\text{gn}} $, respectively\footnote{The subscript $gn$ stands for Gaussian noise and is justified to avoid confusion with both the parameters $\mu$ - used to denote the viscosity of the fluid - and $\mD$ - used to denote the dual field of the  compatibility equation.}. 

As the maximum and minimum values of both $(\tilde{\eD}, \tilde{\sD})$ are completely known \textit{a priori}, the normal random noised generated for both data field was subjected to an min-max scaling process in order to adjust the noise range of data noise to be compatible with that found in the manufactured solution. Mathematical, we can express the noise data fields as:

\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\tilde{\eD}|_{\text{noise}} &=  \mathbf{g}_{\text{scaling}}  (\max(\tilde{\eD}) - \min(\tilde{\eD}) ) + \min(\tilde{\eD}), \ \mathbf{g}_{\text{scaling}} = \dfrac{\mathbf{g}_{\text{gn}} - \min(\textbf{g}_{gn})}{\max(\textbf{g}_{gn}) - \min(\textbf{g}_{gn})} \\
		\tilde{\sD}|_{\text{noise}} &=  \mathbf{s}_{\text{scaling}}  (\max(\tilde{\sD}) - \min(\tilde{\sD}) ) + \min(\tilde{\sD}), \quad \mathbf{s}_{\text{scaling}} = \dfrac{\mathbf{s}_{\text{gn}} - \min(\textbf{s}_{gn})}{\max(\textbf{s}_{gn}) - \min(\textbf{s}_{gn})} \\
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

where, $ \mathbf{g}_{\text{gn}} $ and $ \mathbf{s}_{\text{gn}} $ are vectors whose components $ g_{i} $ and $ s_{i} $ respectively, are independently drawn from a univariate normal distribution, i.e. $ g_{i} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\text{gn}},\sigma_{\text{gn}})$ and $ s_{i} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\text{gn}},\sigma_{\text{gn}}), \forall i \in \{1,\dots,d\}$. Finally, in order to control the magnitude of generated noisy data, we introduce a scalar parameter, $ \alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, which multiplies $ \tilde{\eD}|_{\text{noise }} $ and $ \tilde{\sD}|_{\text{noise}} $, so from now on we'll denote $ \alpha $ as a noise control factor. 


In the first experiment with noisy data fields, three different values of the pair $ (N_{m},N_{\theta}) $ were considered, and the following parameters $ \mu_{\text{gn}} = 0.0 $, $ \sigma_{\text{gn}} = 1.0 $ and $ \alpha = 2.5 \times 10^{-2} $ were adopted to introduce the random normal noise data. Figure \ref{fig:orig_flux_grad_samples} illustrates the relationship between $ \|\tilde{\sD} \|_{2} $ and $ \|\tilde{\eD}\|_{2} $ for the three selected values of $ (N_{m},N_{\theta}) $. The choice for those values intends to characterize distinct data scenarios: the first one (figure \ref{fig:orig_flux_grad_samples_NbcNth_4}) should represent absence (or lack) of data; the second one (figure \ref{fig:orig_flux_grad_samples_NbcNth_8}) should represent an intermediate case, where an reasonable number os points is provided, and the last one (figure \ref{fig:orig_flux_grad_samples_NbcNth_16}) should depict an ideal case, where a large amount of data is given.
